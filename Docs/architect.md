# War Game — Basic Prototype Architecture

> Source: [wishlist.md](wishlist.md). Implementation steps: [plan.md](plan.md).
> This is a **small, hardcoded prototype**. The goal is to prove that "fly a jet" and "command from a map" are fun together. Everything else waits.

---

## 1. What the prototype is

One small battlefield on a stretch of coast. You are the NATO commander and also a fighter pilot.

- **Flying (main mode):** take off from your airbase in a jet, fight enemy jets with guns and heat-seeking missiles, strafe enemy ground units, and land to refuel and rearm. G-forces, fuel leaks and big explosions.
- **Map (press M):** a top-down view of the same world. You see all units as icons, select your units and order them to move. Ground units fight automatically using simple strength numbers.
- **Win:** destroy or drive off all enemy ground units, or capture the enemy base. **Lose:** the enemy captures your base, or you run out of jets.

## 2. Decisions

| # | Decision | Choice |
|---|----------|--------|
| D1 | Scope | Jet + simple map + infrastructure (§6.4) + infantry mode (§6.5) + ballistic warheads (§6.7). No economy, fog of war, satellites, radio delays, carrier or navy. |
| D2 | Simulation scale | **No LOD system.** Jets are always individual entities (few of them). Ground units are always aggregated: one icon/marker = one unit with a strength number. |
| D3 | Map | A **mini Earth, 128 km × 128 km**, with Europe magnified (~40 km across) as the only front: continents and islands, ~20 rivers, straits, mountain ranges, deserts and forests, a road network between real cities, and a bridge wherever a road crosses water. NATO airbase in Germany, enemy airbase east of Moscow. Terrain from a single heightmap image generated from hand-authored coastlines (`geo.rs`). |
| D4 | Flight | Semi-arcade aero model (lift/drag/thrust, stall, G-limit) + low-poly art. |
| D5 | Players | Single-player vs simple AI. |
| D6 | Time | Real time. **Esc** pauses everything. No speed-up. The war keeps running while you fly or use the map. |
| D7 | Player death | The jet is lost. You return to the map and launch your next jet from the reserve (start with 4). |
| D8 | Tech | Rust + Bevy (pinned exact version). Minimal crates. No physics library: terrain collision = height lookup. |

## 4. World

- **Size:** 128 × 128 km, a stylised Earth. f32 precision is still fine (mm at the edges), so no floating origin.
- **Projection (`geo.rs`):** everything is authored in longitude/latitude and mapped by a piecewise-linear warp: ~0.7 km per degree of longitude and 1 km per degree of latitude over Europe, squeezed elsewhere. The Americas, Asia, Africa and Australia are real terrain you can fly to, but the war is in Europe.
- **Terrain:** a 1025² heightmap (125 m cells) generated by `examples/gen_heightmap.rs`: coastline polygons are rasterised into a signed distance field, roughened with noise, raised inland, plus Gaussian mountain ridges (Alps, Carpathians, Urals, Caucasus, Himalaya, Rockies, Andes...), carved river valleys and straits, and flattened runways. Split into 16 × 16 flat-shaded chunks coloured by climate (desert, tundra, snow line), forest and slope; sea floor under the opaque sea is skipped.
- **Height queries:** `height at (x, z)` and `is water` are pure functions used for collision, ground-unit placement and AI.
- **Sea:** a flat water plane at sea level. **Sky:** Bevy's sky/atmosphere if available, otherwise a clear color + fog.
- **Rivers and straits:** ~20 rivers in Europe (Rhine, Elbe, Oder, Vistula, Neman, Daugava, Dnieper, Danube, Don, Volga, ...) and more elsewhere, each a polyline with a gentle meander, plus carved straits (Gibraltar, Bosphorus, Dardanelles, Øresund, Great Belt, Fehmarn Belt, Dover, Messina, Kerch, Suez).
- **Roads:** ~30 roads through ~60 cities (`geo::ROADS`), drawn as ribbons on the terrain. Units on a road move 1.8× faster and path finding prefers them.
- **Bridges:** found automatically wherever a road crosses water no wider than 2 km (~40 bridges). Each has a deck from bank to bank with an arch, pillars, and its own HP; destroying one cuts the road for everyone.
- **Forests:** a noise field limited by climate (none in deserts, tundra or high mountains). In the theatre ~60 000 low-poly trees are merged into 4 km chunk meshes that hide beyond 14 km. Forest slows vehicles, shelters infantry units in combat, and gives the player on foot cover (§6.5).
- **Hardcoded places:**
  - NATO airbase (central Germany): runway strip flattened into the heightmap, a few hangar boxes, a "base zone" circle.
  - Enemy airbase (east of Moscow): same.
  - A power plant, fuel depot and radar per side (§6.4).
- **Units and conventions:** meters, seconds, kg, radians. +Y up, −Z forward (Bevy default).

## 5. Air and combat (flight mode)

### 5.1 Jet flight model
Runs in `FixedUpdate` at 60 Hz for every jet (player and AI share the same code).
- **Forces:** thrust (throttle 0–100%, afterburner above 90%), lift = ½ρv²S·C_L(AoA) with a simple stall drop-off, drag = ½ρv²S·(C_D0 + k·C_L² + gear/airbrake), gravity. Air density from a simple exponential atmosphere.
- **Rotation:** stick input → target pitch/roll/yaw rates, scaled by airspeed (sluggish when slow), capped by a **G-limiter**.
- **G-force:** load factor from lift ÷ weight. A tolerance meter fills above ~7 G → grey vignette → blackout (controls fade). Below −2.5 G → red-out.
- **Fuel:** burns by throttle. Empty → engine out (glide). **Fuel leak:** a hit can puncture the tank, causing a leak rate and a HUD warning.
- **Ground:** wheel contact against terrain/runway height, brakes, and a crash if the jet touches down too hard or with gear up.
- **Two jet types**, hardcoded stats: NATO fighter (F-35-like) and enemy fighter (Su-35-like). Same model, different numbers.

### 5.2 Damage
- Each jet has **HP** plus three simple flags/values: engine damaged (less thrust), fuel tank hit (leak), on fire (slow HP drain). That's enough to make damaged jets limp home.
- Ground units take damage as **strength loss** (§6.2).

### 5.3 Weapons
| Weapon | Model |
|--------|-------|
| Cannon | Fast projectiles with gravity. Each tick, check the segment it travelled against target hit-spheres and terrain. Tracers. |
| Heat-seeking missile | Lock when the target is inside the seeker cone and range (lock tone). Proportional-navigation guidance, burn then coast, max turn G, proximity fuse. 4 per jet. |
| Flares | Limited count. Each flare has a chance to decoy a missile that is tracking you. |
| Rockets/bombs vs ground | **Optional stretch goal.** Strafing ground units with the cannon is enough at first. |

- **Detonation:** one shared "explode here (position, size)" message. It deals radius damage to jets/ground units and triggers FX. All explosions go through it.
- Missile warning: a tone + HUD arrow when an enemy missile is tracking you.

### 5.4 Airbase restock
Stop on your own runway for 5 s → refuel, rearm and repair over ~10 s (HUD progress bar). This covers the wishlist's "land and takeoff to restock".

### 5.5 Enemy and wingman AI (pilots)
A small state machine per AI jet, controlling the **same stick/throttle inputs** as the player:
- **Patrol:** fly between waypoints at cruise altitude.
- **Engage:** turn toward the nearest visible enemy jet. Fire a missile when locked and in range. Use guns when close.
- **Evade:** when a missile is tracking it, break turn + flares.
- **Return to base:** low fuel/ammo or heavily damaged → fly home and land (simplified: once over its base below 300 m, it despawns and returns to the pool after a cooldown).
- **Terrain avoidance:** pull up if predicted height above terrain < 200 m.
- The enemy keeps **2–4 jets** airborne. Friendly AI flights (1–2 jets) also exist and obey map orders.

## 6. Map mode and ground units

### 6.1 Map view (press M)
- Same 3D world, high top-down camera. Pan with WASD/edge-drag, zoom with scroll. Press M again to return to the jet.
- **Icons** (billboards that stay readable at any zoom): ground units (NATO-style rectangle with a strength bar), jets (small plane icons with heading), airbases, bridges (red cross when down), roads, city names, objective zones. Battles show as a flashing crossed-swords icon. Zoom out far enough to see the whole world.
- **Interaction:** left-click selects a friendly unit (ground unit or friendly AI flight). Right-click sets a move destination (ground units) or patrol point (flights). The selected unit's path is drawn as a line.
- **Side panel (egui):** selected unit info with its current orders (Auto / Hold here buttons), jets in reserve, a **Launch Jet** button (only when not currently flying), the warhead silo with a **Launch warhead** button (§6.7), bridges standing, and a short event log ("Vistula bridge near Warsaw destroyed!", "Enemy unit routed").
- The game does **not** pause in map mode. While you're flying, your jet's autopilot holds level flight when the map is open.

### 6.2 Ground units (aggregated)
- About **6 NATO and 8 enemy units** (infantry, armor, anti-air), hardcoded at start.
- Each unit has: type, side, position, destination, **strength** (0–100), attack, defense, speed, and engagement range (~2 km).
- In the 3D world, each unit is shown as a **small cluster of low-poly vehicle/troop models** (e.g. 4–6 tanks). It is purely visual and spread around the unit position. Models disappear as strength drops. Strafing hits any model in the cluster → damage to the unit's strength.
- **Orders:** every unit starts on **Auto**: every ~12 s an idle unit picks the nearest reachable enemy unit (else the enemy airbase, else enemy infrastructure) and advances on it. Anti-air units instead escort the nearest friendly combat unit from 700 m behind. A right-click order overrides Auto; 30 s after the unit arrives it goes back to Auto. **Hold here** keeps it in place until ordered again.
- **Movement:** A* on a 250 m navigation grid (`nav.rs`): land cells, plus bridge cells while the bridge stands. Road cells are cheaper, forest cells dearer for vehicles. The route is snapped onto roads and bridge decks and smoothed without leaving the road. Units are faster on roads (×1.8), slower uphill and through forest (vehicles ×0.55, infantry ×0.85). When a bridge falls, every unit whose route used it re-plans.
- **Combat (once per second):** opposing units within range damage each other: `damage = attacker attack × (attacker strength/100) × random(0.8–1.2) ÷ defender defense`. Infantry in forest takes 30% less. Strength 0 → destroyed. Below 25 → retreats toward its own base.
- **Anti-air units** shoot at enemy jets within ~5 km. A simple chance per second spawns a real heat-seeking missile at the jet, so the player has to dodge them.
- **Enemy ground "AI":** the same Auto behaviour, in two waves: wave 1 in Belarus at the start, wave 2 near Moscow after 5 minutes, which only sets off if it still has a route west (blow the bridges to stall it).

### 6.4 Infrastructure
- Each side has a **power plant**, a **fuel depot** and a **radar**, hardcoded in `config.rs`. Each is a low-poly model with one hit sphere relayed to the site, and HP. Bullets do 35% damage; blasts do full damage. Destroyed → rubble and smoke.
- **Effects** (power feeds the other two):
  - Radar down (or no power) → that side's AA fires at 40% of the normal rate, its AI jets detect targets at half range, and for NATO the map only shows enemy jets within 8 km of your own forces.
  - Fuel down (or no power) → NATO restock takes 2.5× longer; enemy jets launch 2.5× less often.
- **Who attacks them:** the player (guns, unguided missiles, drone bombs); ground units with no enemy unit in range shell enemy sites in range; and **enemy strike raids**, where every ~2.5 min one enemy fighter switches to a `Strike` state, runs in low, dives and fires 2 guided missiles at a NATO site (75 damage each), then breaks off. Raids are announced in the event log and drawn on the map.
- Map: site icons (P/F/R) with HP bars; the side panel lists every site and the active effects.

### 6.5 Infantry mode
- Infantry units are drawn as squads of **individually modeled soldiers** (still one aggregated unit with a strength number; no LOD system).
- **Joining:** select a NATO infantry unit on the map and press **I** (or the panel button). You appear behind the squad in first person. **M** returns to the map (the soldier rejoins the squad). If you are flying at the time, the jet keeps flying on autopilot.
- **On foot:** walk/sprint/crouch on the heightmap and bridge deck, no swimming. Rifle tracers use the shared bullet code (hits on unit models cost strength), with a magazine, reload, aim zoom, and ammo resupply near friendly units.
- **Threats:** once a second every enemy unit in range with terrain line of sight may hit you. Crouching cuts the chance to 35%; being spotted by an **enemy recon drone** multiplies it by 2.5. Drones circle the enemy front and hunt you when you are near it. They see you unless you are crouched and still, and you can shoot them down. Jet guns and blasts hurt you too. When you die: death cam → map, and the squad loses 3 strength. Soldier lives are free; only jets are limited.
- **IR drone (T):** a quadcopter you fly (WASD, Q/E height, mouse look) within 2.5 km for 60 s. Enemy troops and vehicles glow white. LMB drops one of 2 small bombs on the marked spot. Then it recharges for 40 s.

### 6.7 Ballistic warheads
- Each side has a silo on its mainland, on the mini Earth: NATO in the American Midwest (4 warheads), the enemy in western Siberia (3).
- **Launch Warhead** on the map panel, then left-click the impact point (right-click cancels). One launch per 60 s per side.
- The missile flies a visible ballistic arc (apex 30% of the distance, 6–24 km), taking 12 s + 1 s per 2.2 km (~35 s from America to Poland). The map shows its track, the 650 m blast circle and a countdown.
- Impact: the shared detonation (340 damage, 650 m radius) wrecks units, bridges, sites, jets and the soldier, with a mushroom cloud, a ground shock ring, a long-range flash and camera shake.
- The enemy fires every ~4 min at the biggest NATO unit cluster (else a NATO site), with a warning in the event log, so you have ~25 s to scatter.

### 6.6 Win and lose
- **Capture:** a base is captured if enemy ground units sit in its zone with no defenders for 30 s.
- **Win:** all enemy ground units destroyed, or the enemy base captured.
- **Lose:** your base captured, or zero jets left in reserve while not flying.
- End screen with simple stats: kills, losses, time.

## 7. Player and views

- **Game states:** Menu → Playing → (Paused) → GameOver.
- **View modes (while Playing):** Flight (cockpit or chase, toggle **V**), Map (**M**), Infantry (first person on foot, **I** from the map), DeathCam (3 s after being shot down or killed, then Map).
- **Infantry controls:** WASD move, Shift sprint, C crouch, mouse look, LMB fire, RMB aim, R reload, T IR drone.
- **Flight controls (keyboard + mouse):** W/S pitch, A/D roll, Q/E yaw, Shift/Ctrl throttle, Space/LMB guns, RMB missile, X flares, G gear, B airbrake, mouse for free-look in cockpit.
- **HUD (bevy_ui):** speed, altitude, heading, G meter, throttle, fuel (+ leak warning), ammo/missiles/flares, a pitch line, target box + lock indicator, missile warning, a "restocking" progress bar.
- **Cockpit:** a simple low-poly frame around the view. Chase cam behind the jet.

---

## 8. FX and audio (minimal but punchy)

- **Explosions:** pooled camera-facing billboard particles (fireball, smoke, sparks, a few debris chunks with gravity), a brief point-light flash and camera shake scaled by size and distance. Four sizes: hit spark, missile/vehicle, jet crash/bridge, warhead (mushroom cloud).
- Missile smoke trails, tracers, afterburner glow.
- **Audio:** engine hum (pitch by throttle), gun, missile launch, explosion, lock tone, missile warning. All built-in Bevy audio.

## 9. Code layout

A single Cargo binary. One Bevy **plugin** per file/folder. Plugins talk through components, resources and messages/events.

```
war-game/
├─ Cargo.toml
├─ assets/
│  ├─ heightmap.png
│  ├─ icons/          unit/jet/base icons
│  └─ audio/
└─ src/
   ├─ main.rs         app setup, plugin list
   ├─ state.rs        game states, view modes, pause
   ├─ config.rs       ALL hardcoded numbers: jet stats, weapons,
   │                  unit stats, starting units, base positions
   ├─ geo.rs          the mini Earth: projection, coastlines, rivers, straits,
   │                  mountains, climate, cities, roads, bridge finder
   ├─ heightgen.rs    heightmap generator (also used by the example)
   ├─ world.rs        heightmap, terrain chunks, sea, sky, bases, bridges,
   │                  road ribbons, trees
   ├─ nav.rs          road index, navigation grid, A* path finding
   ├─ flight.rs       flight model, G-force, fuel/leaks, ground contact, restock
   ├─ weapons.rs      cannon, missiles, flares, detonation, damage
   ├─ pilot_ai.rs     AI jet state machine, enemy jet spawning
   ├─ ground.rs       ground units, auto orders, movement, auto-combat, AA fire,
   │                  enemy waves
   ├─ infrastructure.rs  power plants, fuel depots, radars, effects, strike raids
   ├─ infantry.rs     on-foot soldier, rifle, IR drone, recon drones, infantry HUD
   ├─ map.rs          map camera, icons, selection, orders, side panel
   ├─ player.rs       jet reserve, launch, death, camera rigs, input
   ├─ hud.rs          flight HUD
   ├─ fx.rs           explosions, trails, camera shake
   ├─ audio.rs        sounds
   ├─ warheads.rs     silos, ballistic warheads, enemy launches
   ├─ game_rules.rs   capture, win/lose, end screen
   └─ debug.rs        overlays and cheats (F-keys)
```

**Crates:** `bevy` (pinned), `bevy_egui` (map side panel/debug sliders, only if it supports the pinned Bevy version), `rand`. Nothing else.

## 10. Budgets, risks, testing

- **Budgets:** ≤ 12 jets, ≤ 20 ground units (≤ 150 visual models; an infantry model is 5 soldiers), 6 sites, ~40 bridges, ~60 000 trees (merged per chunk), ≤ 500 bullets, ≤ 30 missiles. 60 FPS (measured ~170 FPS on an RX 5700 XT).
- **Biggest risk: the flight model doesn't feel good.** Build it first and expose its constants as debug sliders.
- **Second risk: map and flight feel disconnected.** That's why the player can strafe ground units, AA shoots at the player, and the bridges matter.
- **Tests:** unit tests for the pure math (atmosphere, lift/drag, G, missile guidance, ground combat formula, terrain height lookup, projection, path finding across the mini Earth and over bridges, warhead arcs). Everything else is checked by playing, with debug overlays (velocity/lift vectors, missile lines, unit ranges) and cheats (infinite fuel, god mode, spawn enemy jet).
